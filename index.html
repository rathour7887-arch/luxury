Philosophy

We are not in the business of feeding people. We are in the business of an hour they cannot get back — and would not want to.

We do not serve dinner. We stage a single, unrepeatable hour of your life — and ask that you arrive with nowhere else to be.

There is no printed menu in advance, no substitutions, and no second seating. The room holds fourteen guests. The kitchen holds nine courses, decided the morning of, by what the coast, the forest, and the cellar have agreed to give up. Everything else — the light, the silence between courses, the pace of the evening — is composed, not served.

The Kitchen

Chef Adrian Voss

Adrian Voss spent eleven years cooking in rooms with no windows before opening one with none by design. He does not believe a great meal should be photographed, only remembered — imperfectly, the way the best evenings always are.

Every tasting menu is written the same morning it is served, built entirely around what arrived at the back door before sunrise. There is no version of Nocturne's menu that will ever exist twice.

"The dish is not the point. The hour is."
